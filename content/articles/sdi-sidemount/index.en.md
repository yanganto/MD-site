When technical diving calls for more than one cylinder and you don't have a setup ready at every dive site, sidemount is a common solution.
Yet recreational sidemount with just a single cylinder avoids the frequent hands-on task of switching between two cylinders, and it has plenty of other advantages.

1. It's compact and easy to carry. A single suitcase can hold my BCD, regulators, scuba fins, drysuit, and mask — and still have room for more. On a dive trip to Mexico, I packed free-dive fins, a snorkel, and several sets of everyday clothes on top of my gear, and even for a dive trip of over 14 days, I still only needed one suitcase.
2. With the first stage positioned at the front, there's no stress about forgetting to open a cylinder, you can just open it after entering the water. Shooting with a 360 camera on a short leash also eliminates the risk of tangling with the first stage.
3. A more streamlined profile makes it less likely to snag in wrecks or caves.
4. Pairs better with a tech pack, letting you carry more. Every dive I bring a clipboard, a pen, a compass, a mirror, a line spool, an SMB, one action camera, and one 360 camera.

SDI sidemount doesn't demand strict technical requirements. Once you're familiar with filling and venting the cylinder and entering/exiting the water, it's a lighter and more flexible rig than a backplate or jacket setup.
